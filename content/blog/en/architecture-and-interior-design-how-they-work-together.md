When you think about a home, you might start with how it looks from the outside or with how you would like to live in its rooms. When preparing a commission, it helps to bring both perspectives together: what you need, what relationship you want with the surroundings and how you want to feel in each space.

Talking about **architecture and interior design** through these questions lets you approach the project without separating your practical needs from your personal preferences. It does not mean every type of work is automatically included: the scope of each commission should be made clear.

## One home, different design questions

To organise your ideas, you can think in two groups of questions, without turning them into separate compartments.

On one side are the questions about the house as a whole: how it is organised, how it relates to the outdoors and the proportion between its spaces. On the other are those closer to daily life: how you want to use a room, what atmosphere you are looking for, and what you need in terms of privacy and storage.

The same decision can belong to both groups. If you would like a living area that opens onto the garden, for example, it is worth explaining both that spatial preference and how you imagine using it. Is it a place to gather? Would you also like a quieter corner? These are questions to explore, not solutions that need to be decided in advance.

## Start with your habits, not only with images

Visual references can help you express your taste, but try to explain them. Rather than simply pointing out "I like this living room", identify what interests you: its openness, its sense of calm, its proportions or the relationship between different areas.

To prepare that conversation, note down:

- **Who will use the house, and how.** Describe your routines and the situations you want to plan for.
- **Which activities need their own space.** Working, resting or receiving guests, for example, if they are part of your needs.
- **What feelings you are looking for.** Privacy, openness or a sense of retreat can be a starting point.
- **What matters most.** Separate what is essential from what you would like to consider.

You do not need to arrive with a finished layout. You can share needs, preferences and doubts without anticipating a specific solution.

## Connect the design with the setting and sustainability

Martínez Galván approaches architecture with criteria of sustainability, respect for the setting, balance and proportion. To discuss these principles in your project, it helps to turn them into concrete questions.

You can ask how the relationship with the site is approached, which criteria guide the design decisions and how different alternatives are assessed. If you have a preference about materials or an expectation about energy use, say so, so that it can be studied, without assuming a particular performance in advance.

**A sustainability criterion is not, on its own, a measured result or a certification.** Avoid reading a design intention as a guarantee about how the house will perform in the future.

## Be clear about what the commission includes

Martínez Galván offers architecture, interior design and the coordination of projects that combine both. Before moving forward, ask how that combination applies to your case.

These questions can be useful in the conversation:

1. Which needs will the commission address?
2. Which work is included, and which is not?
3. What documentation is planned?
4. How will architecture and interior design decisions relate to each other?
5. What fees and programme are proposed for that scope?

Do not assume that decoration, purchasing or furnishing work is included simply because interior design is part of the conversation. It is better to raise each need and agree its scope.

## A starting point for your project on the Costa del Sol

If you are preparing a home in Marbella or elsewhere on the Costa del Sol, start with a short document setting out your priorities, references and questions. It does not need to be a design proposal: it is enough for it to explain what you expect from your future home.
