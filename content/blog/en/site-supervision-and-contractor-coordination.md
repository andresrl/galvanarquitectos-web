Once building starts, many people are involved: the architect, the main contractor, the different trades, the suppliers. Understanding what each of them does is one of the best ways to avoid misunderstandings and to know who to turn to at each moment.

This guide explains in general terms what site supervision and contractor coordination usually involve, and what to clarify in your own commission.

## Designing is not the same as building

The architect designs the project and, if the commission includes it, supervises the works. The contractor and specialist companies carry out the work. These are complementary roles, but they are different.

Martínez Galván offers design, planning permissions, site supervision and contractor coordination according to the agreed scope. The studio does not act as the builder: the companies that carry out the works are engaged separately.

## What site supervision involves

Site supervision means following the works so that what is built matches the project. In practice, it involves visiting the site, answering questions that come up during construction, checking that solutions are carried out as planned and guiding decisions when something unforeseen appears.

The exact scope, the frequency of visits and the responsibilities are agreed for each commission. Ask about them at the start.

## What contractor coordination involves

Many trades work on a villa, at the same time or one after another. Coordination helps their work fit together and with the project: each company has the information it needs, and one company's decisions do not compromise another's.

When architecture, interiors and landscape are part of the same commission, coordination helps keep a shared vision from start to finish.

## What to clarify at the start

1. Is site supervision included in the commission?
2. What exactly does contractor coordination cover?
3. Who engages the contractor and the trades?
4. How often will the site be visited?
5. How are changes or unforeseen issues communicated?
6. How will I be kept informed if I am not in Marbella?

## What not to assume

- That the architect carries out or engages the works, if that has not been agreed.
- That coordination includes purchasing, supplies or furniture.
- That changes during the works will not affect cost or timing.

Clarifying these points is not a sign of distrust: it means everyone works with the same expectations.
