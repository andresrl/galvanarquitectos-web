A new villa starts long before the first sketch. It starts on the plot: with how the sun falls on it, how you arrive, what you can see from it and what can be built there. Understanding these aspects early avoids designing something that does not fit the place.

This guide sets out the questions worth asking. It does not replace the technical study or the review of planning rules, which must be carried out for each specific plot.

## Orientation and light

Orientation shapes where the main rooms, terraces and pool are best placed. It is not only about finding sun: it is also about controlling heat in summer, making the most of light in winter and deciding which spaces need shade.

Visit the plot at different times of day if you can. Morning light and evening light tell different stories.

## Access and arrival

Where do you come in? How do cars, guests and services arrive? Access influences the organisation of the whole house: where the main entrance sits, how private areas are separated and how the home relates to the street.

On sloping plots, the arrival point may be at a different level from the main rooms. It is worth knowing this from the start.

## Topography

A flat plot and a sloping one call for different projects. A slope can offer views and interesting changes of level, but it also requires walls, terraces and access to be resolved. It is neither better nor worse: it is a starting condition that needs to be studied.

## Views and privacy

Views are often one of the reasons for choosing a plot. At the same time, what you see from the house may also be seen from outside. It is worth studying what you want to look at, what you want to protect and how the plot relates to neighbouring properties.

## What is already on the plot

Trees, earlier buildings, walls or existing changes of level are part of the place. Some are worth keeping; others will shape the design. Note them down and ask how they can be integrated.

## Planning rules

What can be built on a plot depends on the applicable planning rules: floor areas, heights, distances to boundaries and other conditions. These rules vary by municipality and area, and private estates may also have their own community rules.

**Do not assume how much can be built on a plot** from what has been built next door or from what an advert says. It needs to be checked for each case.

## Questions for your first conversation

1. What is the plot's orientation, and what does it mean for the house?
2. How does access shape the organisation of the home?
3. What challenges does the topography raise?
4. What views and what privacy can be achieved?
5. Which planning rules apply, and what needs to be checked?
