Many people who own, or plan to build, a villa on the Costa del Sol live in another country. That does not stop you from taking part in the project, but it does mean organising a few things more carefully: how decisions are communicated, when it is worth being there in person and what information you need to give an informed opinion.

This guide does not describe a fixed system. Each commission agrees its own way of working; what matters is defining it at the start.

## Agree how and when you will talk

Distance is easier to manage when communication has a clear rhythm. Before starting, it helps to agree:

- **The main channel.** Video calls to review the design, email to record what has been decided.
- **The frequency.** You do not need to speak every day, but you should know when there will be news.
- **The language.** Martínez Galván works in English and Spanish. Choosing one for the documentation avoids confusion.
- **Who decides.** If several people are involved, make clear who gives final approval on each subject.

Good organisation avoids the feeling of not knowing where the project stands.

## The decisions worth making in person

Many design reviews work well over a video call, with drawings and images shared on screen. Others gain a great deal from being there: understanding the real light on the plot, seeing the view from a room or handling a material sample.

If you are going to travel, try to time your visit with moments when there are decisions to make. A well-prepared visit can settle in a day what would take weeks of messages from a distance.

## Ask for documentation you can review calmly

From abroad you cannot drop by the site whenever you like, so written and visual information is how you follow the project. It is worth agreeing what you will receive and in what format: dated and versioned drawings, proposal images, and summaries of the decisions made at each meeting.

A good habit is to confirm in writing what was agreed on each video call. That way both sides are working from the same information.

## During the works: follow-up and visits

When the project reaches the building stage, the follow-up changes. Architectural site supervision and contractor coordination, if they are part of the commission, make sure what is built matches the project. For you, what matters is knowing how you will hear about progress and when it is useful to be there.

Ask at the outset:

1. How will you keep me informed about progress on site?
2. Which decisions may come up during the works, and how will I be consulted?
3. At which points is it worth visiting the site?
4. Who do I contact if I have an urgent question?

## What not to take for granted

Living abroad can lead you to assume someone will take care of everything. Before starting, be clear about what the commission includes and what it does not: design and site supervision are not the same as handling purchases, supplies or maintenance. Ask about each specific need rather than assuming it.
