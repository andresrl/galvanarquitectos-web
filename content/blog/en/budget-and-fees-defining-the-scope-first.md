"How much will it cost?" is probably the first question on your mind. It is a reasonable one, and it deserves a serious answer. That is exactly why a figure given too early is rarely useful: without knowing what will be done, any number is a guess.

This guide explains why scope comes first and how to prepare a budget conversation that genuinely helps you.

## Two different conversations

It helps to separate two things that are often mixed up:

- **The architect's fees**, which depend on the services included in the commission: design, permissions, site supervision, coordination, interiors or landscape.
- **The cost of the works**, which depends on what is built or renovated, the materials chosen and the companies that carry it out.

Both relate to the scope of the project, but they are not the same. Ask about each one separately.

## Why scope comes first

Two "complete" renovations can be very different projects. One might keep the structure and renew the interiors; another might reorganise the whole house, extend it and rework the garden. Calling them the same does not make them comparable.

Defining the scope means agreeing what will be done, what will not, and in how much detail. Only then does it make sense to estimate costs and compare options.

## What you can bring from the start

Even before there are figures, you can do a lot to steer the project:

1. **Your priorities.** What is essential and what you would like to consider.
2. **Your investment range.** If you have a range in mind, sharing it helps produce realistic proposals.
3. **Your references.** Images or examples you like, explaining what appeals to you.
4. **Your constraints.** Personal dates, use of the house during the process, or decisions you have already made.

Sharing an investment range is not a commitment. It is information that lets the design respond to reality.

## Questions worth asking

- Which services do the fees include, and which do they not?
- How will the scope be defined before costs are estimated?
- At what point in the project can we talk about figures more precisely?
- Which decisions have the greatest influence on the final cost?
- How are changes that arise during the project handled?

## Be wary of figures without context

A price per square metre or a fixed figure before anyone has visited the house may feel reassuring, but it rarely reflects your actual project. A clear process that lets you make informed decisions at each stage is more useful.
