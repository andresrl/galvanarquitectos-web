Permits are often one of the biggest concerns when starting a building project. That is understandable: being able to build or renovate depends on them, and the requirements are not always obvious if you do not work in the sector. For international clients, a different system and language can add to the uncertainty.

This guide does not explain the rules of any particular municipality, because they change with the location and the type of work. What it does is help you prepare the questions worth taking to your architect.

## Why every case is different

Not all works go through the same process. The type of work (a new build, an extension, an interior renovation), the municipality, the area and the particular conditions of the plot all play a part. Private estates may also have their own community rules.

That is why it is wise to be wary of general answers such as "that is always allowed" or "that always takes the same time". The prudent approach is to study each case.

## What the architect does in this process

As part of the architectural commission, the project documentation needed to apply for the relevant permits is prepared. Handling those applications can form part of the commission, depending on what is agreed.

Ask at the start which part of the process your commission covers and which part, if any, falls to you or to other professionals.

## Questions worth asking

1. **What type of permit does my project need?** It depends on what you want to do and where.
2. **What documentation has to be prepared?** And who prepares it.
3. **Are there community rules as well as municipal ones?** Especially on private estates.
4. **Which aspects of the design depend on the planning rules?** Heights, floor areas, distances or uses can shape the project.
5. **What can affect the timescale?** Not to get a fixed date, but to understand what it depends on.
6. **What happens if I want to change something during the works?** Some changes may require the documentation to be updated.

## About timescales

It is natural to want to know how long it will take. The honest answer is that it depends on factors neither the architect nor the client fully controls, such as the administration's processing times or the need to provide additional documentation.

More useful than a promised date is understanding the stages of the process and planning with some margin.

## What to avoid

- **Starting work before it is clear which permits are needed.**
- **Assuming that what a neighbour did is possible on your plot.**
- **Making final design decisions without knowing the planning conditions.**
