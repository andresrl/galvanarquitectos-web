When a villa no longer fits the way you live, a big question appears: is it worth renovating, or is it better to start again? There is no general answer. It depends on the house, the plot, what you need and what the planning rules allow in that location.

What you can do is structure the decision with good questions before leaning towards one option.

## Start with what works and what does not

Before talking about solutions, make an honest list about your current house:

- **What you like and want to keep.** An orientation, a view, a mature garden, a particular room.
- **What does not work.** A layout that does not suit your life, dark spaces, a poor connection with the outdoors.
- **What is missing.** More bedrooms, a place to work, rooms for receiving guests.

If there is a lot you value and the problems are concentrated in certain spaces, a renovation can make a great deal of sense. If almost everything you need means changing the structure of the house, the question changes.

## What needs to be studied in the existing house

A renovation starts from what is already built, so it helps to understand its condition and possibilities before deciding. Some questions can only be answered with a visit and a study of the house: how the structure is organised, which layout changes are reasonable and how the house relates to its plot.

Do not take anything for granted in either direction: neither that everything can be changed, nor that nothing can be touched.

## What needs to be studied on the plot

The decision does not depend on the building alone. The planning rules that apply to each plot determine what can be built, extended or altered. Those conditions vary from place to place and need to be checked for each specific case.

So before comparing options, ask what room for manoeuvre your plot offers. That information can tip the balance more clearly than any aesthetic preference.

## Beyond the building: how you want to live the process

Renovating and building new are not experienced in the same way. It is worth asking yourself:

1. Which part of the current house has special value for you?
2. Do you want to keep the character of the house, or are you looking for something different?
3. How does each option fit your plans and your personal calendar?
4. Do you need to keep using the house at any point during the process?

These are not technical questions, but they help the decision reflect your real situation.

## The budget comes after the scope

It is natural to want to compare figures from day one. However, the cost of a renovation or a new build depends on what is decided. Before comparing budgets, it helps to define the scope of each option. That way you compare equivalent projects rather than impressions.
