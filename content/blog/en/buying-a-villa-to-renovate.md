Many villas on the Costa del Sol are bought to be transformed: an older house in a good location can become a contemporary home. Whether that works depends on things that are not always visible on a viewing. Looking at them with an architect before you commit helps you understand what the renovation may involve.

The studio does not sell or broker property. This guide is about the architectural questions; legal and financial checks belong to your lawyer and advisers.

## The structure

Cracks, damp, deformation or previous alterations can indicate how the structure behaves. A first visual inspection tells you whether a specialist assessment is advisable before going further, especially if you plan to open up walls or add a floor.

## Installations

Electricity, plumbing, drainage, heating and cooling, and the pool installations are often renewed in a complete renovation. Knowing their condition helps to estimate the scope of the works, even before talking about figures.

## The layout and its potential

Some houses accept a new layout easily; others are organised around walls or levels that are difficult to change. Ask what can be opened, connected or extended, and how the relationship between rooms, terraces and garden could improve.

## What was built and what was authorised

Before planning changes, it is worth checking that what exists matches what was authorised. Extensions, enclosed terraces or outbuildings that were never legalised can condition the renovation. Your lawyer and the planning records will confirm it; the architect can tell you what to look for.

## What the plot allows

If you plan to extend, the applicable planning rules decide what is possible: built area, heights, distances to boundaries and, in some developments, community rules. Do not assume it from the neighbouring houses.

## Orientation, views and garden

A renovation can rethink how a house relates to the sun, the views and the garden. Visit at different times of day and look at the outdoor spaces as part of the project, not as an afterthought.

## Questions before you commit

1. Does the structure need a specialist assessment?
2. Which installations will probably need renewing?
3. How flexible is the layout?
4. Does what exists match what was authorised?
5. Is an extension possible under the applicable rules?
