Benahavís lies in the hills behind the coast, and many of its plots have a slope. A slope can offer views, privacy and interesting levels, but it changes how a villa is designed and built. These are the aspects worth studying from the start.

They are questions, not conclusions: every plot is different and must be studied on site.

## Where you arrive

On a slope, the access road can reach the plot above or below the main rooms. That decides where the entrance is, how cars and visitors arrive and how many levels the house needs to connect. It is one of the first things to understand.

## Levels and terraces

The house can follow the land in several levels or sit on a platform. Each option changes the relationship between rooms, terraces, pool and garden, and the amount of earth to move. Stepped terraces often turn the slope into outdoor rooms.

## Retaining walls and the ground

Building on a slope usually involves retaining walls and foundations adapted to the ground. A **geotechnical study** of the plot is part of the project and tells the structure what it can rely on. It is better to know its results before the design is fixed.

## Water

Rainwater on a slope needs a path. Drainage of the plot, the terraces and the walls is studied together with the landscape design, so that the garden and the house work with it, not against it.

## Views and privacy

Height brings views, but also visibility from neighbouring plots and the road. The design decides what to frame, what to protect and how the house reads from outside.

## The garden on a slope

Walls, planting and paths can make a sloping garden usable and pleasant. Native and drought-tolerant planting is often considered, but the choice is always made for the specific site.

## Questions for your architect

1. At what level does the access arrive, and what does it mean for the house?
2. How many levels does the design need?
3. When will the geotechnical study be done?
4. How will rainwater be managed?
5. What will be seen from the house, and what from outside?
