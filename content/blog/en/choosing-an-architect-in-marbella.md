Choosing an architect is one of the first decisions of a villa project, and one of the most important. The architect will interpret how you want to live, deal with the site and the regulations, and, in most commissions, follow the works. These are the questions worth asking before you decide.

This guide does not rank studios. It helps you compare them with the same criteria.

## Is the architect qualified to sign the project?

In Spain, the project for a residential building must be drawn up by an architect: Law 38/1999 on Building (LOE) sets that qualification for housing. Ask about the architect's registration with the professional association and who will sign the project.

## Have they designed something comparable?

Look at built projects and proposals of a similar type and scale: a new villa or a complete renovation. Photographs of finished work and architectural visualisations of proposals are both useful, as long as you know which is which.

Ask what the architect would do differently on your plot or in your house. The answer says more than any portfolio.

## Who will you actually talk to?

In some studios, the person you meet is not the person who designs. Ask who will lead your project day to day, who attends the site visits and who answers when a decision is needed.

## What is included in the commission?

Design, planning documentation, site supervision and coordination of the companies involved can be part of one commission or be agreed separately. Define the scope before talking about fees: it is the only way to compare proposals fairly. Our guide on [budget and fees](/journal/budget-and-fees-defining-the-scope-first) explains why.

## Local knowledge

Marbella and its surroundings combine very different settings: established neighbourhoods, residential areas around golf courses, hillside plots and coastal areas, each with its own planning rules. An architect who works locally knows how the municipal procedures work and can visit the property and the site regularly.

## Language and distance

If you live abroad, check how the architect works with clients who are not in Spain: languages, video calls, visits and how the works are followed. Our page for [international clients](/international-clients) describes how the studio does it.

## Questions for a first meeting

1. Who will lead and sign the project?
2. Which comparable projects have you designed?
3. What exactly does the commission include?
4. How will decisions be shared and documented?
5. How often will you be on site during the works?
